Cotton: Where does the journey begin?

Natural resources · Agriculture · Productivity

READ

One farmer. A shocking number of T-shirts.

A simple T-shirt begins with cotton, an agricultural product grown in different parts of the world. However, the country where cotton is grown is not necessarily the country where the shirt is manufactured.

In NPR's investigation, much of the cotton used for its T-shirts came from the United States. American cotton farmers have become highly productive because they use advanced machinery, improved seeds, and technology. These innovations allow farmers to produce large amounts of cotton with fewer workers.

This is an example of how globalization connects different economies. American farmers can grow cotton and sell it to manufacturers in other countries. This creates business opportunities and allows countries to specialize in the products they can produce efficiently.

However, there are also disadvantages. Large-scale cotton farming requires significant amounts of water, land, and energy. The use of pesticides and fertilizers can affect surrounding ecosystems. Additionally, American cotton farmers receive government assistance through subsidies and other programs, which can influence international competition.

Globalization allows businesses to purchase materials from around the world, but it also means that the environmental consequences of production can affect communities far from where the final product is sold.

02

Machines: Technology and the changing world of work

Automation · Capital investment · Efficiency

READ

Sucked up, laid down, and twisted

After cotton is harvested, it must be transformed into yarn before it can become fabric. This process requires specialized machinery, skilled workers, electricity, and factories.

NPR followed the production of its T-shirt to a spinning factory in Indonesia. The factory used expensive machinery capable of operating almost continuously and producing consistent, high-quality yarn.

This demonstrates the importance of capital investment. Businesses invest in machinery and technology because they want to produce goods more quickly and at a lower cost.

Globalization allows companies to invest in factories in different countries and take advantage of local resources, labor, and technology. In Indonesia, manufacturers can produce yarn for international clothing companies, creating jobs and business opportunities.

However, technology can also create challenges. When machines perform tasks previously completed by people, companies may need fewer workers. This is called automation. Some workers may lose their jobs or need additional training to work with newer technology.

There are also environmental concerns. Factories require electricity, and depending on how that electricity is generated, manufacturing can contribute to air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.

Globalization can make production more efficient and affordable, but we must also consider who benefits from these changes and who may be negatively affected.

03

People: Who makes our clothes?

Labor markets · Wages · Working conditions

READ

The lives of the workers who made our shirts

A T-shirt is not produced by machines alone. Thousands of workers around the world contribute their labor to the clothing industry.

NPR's investigation followed garment workers in Bangladesh, including women who worked long hours in factories to support their families. These workers played an essential role in producing clothing for consumers in wealthier countries.

Globalization creates connections between workers in developing countries and businesses in wealthier countries. Clothing companies often choose locations where labor costs are relatively low. This allows businesses to reduce expenses and sell products at competitive prices.

For workers in Bangladesh, garment manufacturing can provide employment opportunities, income, and a chance to support their families. The clothing industry has also created important export opportunities for the country's economy.

However, low wages and difficult working conditions can create serious problems. Some workers face long hours, limited workplace protections, and unsafe factories.

The 2013 Rana Plaza factory collapse in Bangladesh killed more than 1,100 people and exposed serious safety problems in the garment industry. The disaster led to international attention, protests, and pressure for better workplace conditions.

This raises an important question: If globalization creates jobs and lowers clothing prices but workers face unsafe conditions and low wages, is globalization helping or harming people?

04

Boxes: How does a T-shirt travel around the world?

International trade · Transportation · Supply chains

READ

The unsung innovation at the heart of the global economy

A T-shirt may look like a simple product, but its journey from raw material to retail store can involve several countries.

NPR's investigation demonstrated how one T-shirt could begin with cotton grown in the United States, be transformed into yarn in Indonesia, and be assembled into a finished garment in Bangladesh before being shipped to the United States.

How can businesses afford to move products across such great distances? One important innovation was the standardized shipping container. Before containers became widely used, workers had to load and unload individual products and packages from ships. This process was expensive and time-consuming.

Standardized containers made shipping faster, more efficient, and less expensive. Products could be transferred between ships, trains, and trucks without being individually repackaged.

Globalization expanded international trade by making it easier for companies to purchase materials and manufacture products in different countries. Businesses could reduce costs, increase profits, and sell products to customers around the world. Consumers also benefit because international trade makes a wider variety of products available, often at lower prices.

However, shipping products across oceans and continents requires fuel and creates greenhouse gas emissions. International transportation can contribute to climate change and air pollution. Additionally, countries and businesses can become dependent on complicated global supply chains. Disruptions to transportation or manufacturing can delay products and increase costs.

The Port of New York and New Jersey is an important gateway for international trade. Products arriving through the region eventually travel to warehouses, stores, and consumers throughout the metropolitan area.

Globalization makes international trade possible on a massive scale, but the economic benefits must be considered alongside the environmental consequences.

05

You: The consumer and the true cost of a T-shirt

Supply and demand · Consumer choices · Externalities

READ

Where our shirt starts and ends — and everyone in between

After cotton is grown, yarn is manufactured, workers assemble the clothing, and finished products are shipped, the T-shirt finally reaches the consumer.

But what determines the price a customer pays? Businesses consider many factors, including the cost of raw materials, labor, transportation, advertising, and distribution. They also consider how much consumers are willing to pay.

This is where supply and demand become important. If demand for a particular T-shirt increases while supply remains limited, its price may rise. If consumers lose interest or businesses produce too many shirts, prices may fall.

Globalization has helped make clothing more affordable and accessible. Consumers in New York City can purchase shirts produced in countries around the world, often at relatively low prices. This provides more choices and allows families to spend less money on clothing.

However, the price displayed in a store does not necessarily represent the full cost of producing the shirt. Pollution from factories, unsafe working conditions, and environmental damage may create costs that are not reflected in the retail price. Economists call these costs negative externalities.

Consumers also contribute to the problem through fast fashion. When clothing is inexpensive and easy to replace, people may purchase more items than they need and throw them away more frequently. This creates textile waste and increases demand for new production.

Consumers have choices. They can purchase inexpensive clothing, buy secondhand products, or support companies that invest in better labor and environmental practices. Each purchase sends a signal to businesses about what products people want.

Ultimately, globalization connects consumers to workers and environments around the world. The question is whether the benefits of inexpensive clothing and greater consumer choice outweigh the costs to people and the planet.
